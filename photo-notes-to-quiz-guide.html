How to Turn a Photo of Study Notes into a Quiz

Have a page of handwritten or printed notes but no practice questions? A clear photo can be a useful starting point. Extract the text, check it carefully, and then use the text to create a short quiz.

This workflow can save time, but the quality of the questions depends on how readable the photo is and whether you review the extracted text and answers.

1. Take a clear photo

  • Place the page on a flat surface with even lighting.
  • Keep the camera directly above the page and include all four edges.
  • Avoid shadows, glare, blur, and fingers covering the words.
  • Photograph one page at a time if several pages together would make the text too small.

2. Extract the text from the image

Open EduSow Study Materials and choose your image file. Run text extraction, then read through the result before creating questions. Image text recognition can misread handwriting, punctuation, formulas, names, or words in a low-quality photo.

Check first: If the extracted text contains missing lines or incorrect words, fix those errors by comparing it with the original page. Otherwise, the quiz may be based on mistakes.

3. Turn the text into practice questions

Use the checked text with EduSow's Quiz Generator. Begin with five to ten questions about the main ideas. If a question is confusing or unrelated to your notes, do not rely on it—review the source and try again.

  • Answer each question before revealing or checking the answer.
  • Explain to yourself why the correct answer is right.
  • Check factual answers against your original notes or textbook.
  • Make a note of the questions you missed or guessed.

4. Make flashcards for difficult ideas

Not every detail needs a quiz question. Use EduSow Flashcards for short prompts that test one idea at a time, such as a definition, a formula, a cause, or a key difference. Keep the answer concise and correct any generated card that does not match your source.

5. Review what you got wrong

After the quiz, return to the notes for each missed question. Try those questions again later instead of repeatedly reading the entire page. A short follow-up review helps you identify what you still need to learn.

Common problems and fixes

  • Text is missing: Take a sharper photo with better lighting, then extract the text again.
  • Handwriting is hard to read: Type or correct unclear words before generating questions.
  • Questions are too easy or repetitive: Ask for questions on different key ideas and check the result for variety.
  • An answer looks wrong: Verify it against your original notes or a trusted textbook; do not assume AI output is always correct.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make a quiz from a photo of my notes?

Yes. Upload a clear JPG or PNG image to EduSow Study Materials, extract the text, correct any recognition errors, and use the checked text to generate practice questions.

Can I turn handwritten notes into multiple-choice questions?

Yes, if the handwriting is readable enough for text extraction. For unclear words, correct the extracted text manually before generating MCQs.

How do I convert an image into text?

Use an OCR text-extraction tool. OCR means optical character recognition; it identifies text in an image, but it can make mistakes with handwriting, shadows, or blurry photos.

Why are the questions from my photo inaccurate?

The image may be blurry or the extracted text may contain errors. Compare the text with the original notes, fix mistakes, and verify generated answers with a trusted source.

Can I make flashcards from photographed notes too?

Yes. After checking the extracted text, use the flashcard tool to create short question-and-answer cards about definitions, formulas, key facts, and important differences.
