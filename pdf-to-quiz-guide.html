How to Turn a PDF into a Quiz and Flashcards

Reading notes can feel productive, but testing yourself helps reveal what you actually remember. With a simple workflow, you can use a PDF or image of your study notes to create practice questions and flashcards, then check the answers against your original material.

This guide explains the process, how to review the results, and how to avoid relying on AI-generated questions without checking them.

1. Start with a clear, readable document

Choose the chapter or topic you need to revise. A text-based PDF usually works best. If your notes are scanned pages or photos, make sure the image is sharp, upright, and well lit. Handwriting, low resolution, shadows, and complex page layouts can cause text extraction errors.

If the document is long, work on one chapter or a small page range at a time. Smaller sections are easier to check and make practice sessions less overwhelming.

2. Extract the text and check it

Upload the PDF or image to EduSow Study Materials and extract the text. Before generating anything, skim the extracted text for missing lines, incorrect symbols, names, dates, formulas, or words that OCR may have misread. Correct mistakes by referring to your original notes.

Important: Text extraction is not the same as fact-checking. If the extracted text is incomplete or wrong, the quiz and flashcards may also be wrong.

3. Generate a practice quiz

Use the extracted text to generate multiple-choice questions. Start with a manageable number, such as 5–10 questions, and answer them before looking at explanations. Try to explain why the correct option is right and why the other options are wrong.

  • Answer from memory before checking the result.
  • Mark questions you guessed correctly; a lucky guess is not mastery.
  • Return to the textbook for any unclear or disputed answer.
  • Repeat missed questions after reviewing the relevant concept.

4. Turn key ideas into flashcards

Good flashcards test one idea at a time. Put a question, term, formula, or prompt on the front and a short answer on the back. Avoid copying a whole paragraph onto one card. For a difficult concept, make several smaller cards that test its definition, purpose, example, and application separately.

5. Review over several days

Do not wait until the night before a test to review everything. Try a short session the same day, another the next day, and further reviews over the following week. Spend more time on cards you miss and less time on facts you can recall confidently.

  • First session: Learn the ideas and test yourself.
  • Next day: Retry the questions without rereading first.
  • Later in the week: Mix old and new questions.
  • Before an exam: Practise explaining concepts in your own words.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Trusting every AI-generated answer without checking the source.
  • Making flashcards so long that the answer becomes a full page of notes.
  • Rereading repeatedly without testing recall.
  • Studying only the questions you already know.
  • Uploading sensitive documents that you do not have permission to share.

A simple study session you can try today

Choose one topic, extract its text, generate five questions, answer them honestly, and make five flashcards for the ideas you found difficult. Finish by explaining the topic aloud without looking at your notes. Use the mistakes you discover to decide what to revise next.
